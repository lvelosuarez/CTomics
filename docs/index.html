Overview

What is CTomics?

Clinical and environmental metagenomics samples often contain a mix of host DNA and microbial DNA. Before committing to deep sequencing, it is useful to know what fraction of reads will be microbial — this determines how much sequencing you actually need to reach a target number of microbial reads.

CTomics implements the predictive model published by Cho et al. (2021) that links a simple, inexpensive dual-target qPCR result to the percentage of microbial reads in the final sequencing library. No sequencing is needed to obtain the prediction.

The Science

How does the ΔCt model work?

The assay measures two qPCR targets on the same DNA extract: the 16S rRNA gene (universal bacterial marker) and ACTB (human housekeeping gene). Their difference defines the delta Ct:

ΔCt = Ct_ACTB − Ct_16S

A high ΔCt means ACTB amplifies late (little host) and 16S amplifies early (lots of bacteria) — the sample is highly microbial. A low or negative ΔCt indicates host-dominated DNA.

Cho et al. fitted a four-parameter sigmoid curve to 75 clinical samples across a wide range of microbial fractions, achieving R²=0.990. The fitted equation is:

% microbial = 2.7201549 / (99.50267 × e^(−0.7218 × ΔCt) + 0.02733)
Valid range: The model is validated for samples between 4% and 98% microbial reads. Predictions outside this range are flagged but still shown — treat them with caution and consider them indicative only.
Quick Start

How to use the predictor

1

Run the dual-target qPCR

Amplify 16S rRNA and ACTB from your DNA extract using standard qPCR conditions. Record the Ct values for each sample.

2

Calculate ΔCt (or let CTomics do it)

Compute ΔCt = Ct_ACTB − Ct_16S per sample. Alternatively, provide raw Ct values in CSV mode and CTomics will calculate it automatically.

3

Enter your data

Use Manual mode for a quick paste, CSV / Paste to provide a full table, or Upload to load a file directly from disk.

4

Set sequencing parameters

Enter your target microbial read depth (M reads) and total sequencing capacity. CTomics will tell you how much sequencing you need per sample.

5

Click Run Predictions and export

Review results in the table and chart. Download a CSV with all predictions and confidence intervals for your records.

Input Formats

How to format your data

Manual — ΔCt values

One sample per line. Provide an optional ID followed by the ΔCt value, or just raw values.

sample_id   delta_ct AH-PS-001   7.73 S01         20.18 BAL-042     1.20

CSV — Raw Ct values

Provide individual Ct values; CTomics computes ΔCt automatically from ct_actb and ct_16s.

sample_id,ct_actb,ct_16s AH-PS-001,24.881,17.150 HSV-11,29.487,15.288 S01,37.520,17.340
Accepted CSV columns: sample_id, ct_actb, ct_16s — or — sample_id, delta_ct. Column names are case-insensitive. TSV and space-delimited files are also accepted.
Interpreting Results

Understanding the output

Each sample is classified into one of three regimes based on its predicted microbial fraction:

VALID

4–98% microbial. Within the model's validated range. Predictions are reliable. Use the 95% CI to assess uncertainty.

LOW-ΔCt

<4% microbial. Host-dominated sample. The model approaches its lower asymptote. Predictions are indicative — actual microbial fraction may be even lower. Consider host depletion before sequencing.

SATURATED

>98% microbial. Near-pure microbial sample. The sigmoid approaches saturation. Prediction is a lower bound — true fraction is likely at or above 98%.

At N M reads → microbial

How many microbial reads you would obtain if you sequence to your specified total depth. Computed as:
total_depth × (pred% / 100)

To reach target → need

Total sequencing depth required to yield your target number of microbial reads. Computed as:
target_reads / (pred% / 100)
